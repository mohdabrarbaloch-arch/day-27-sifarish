      How it works

      Three signals, one honest blend.

      Item-item collaborative filtering

      People who liked what you liked also liked this. Adjusted cosine similarity with shrinkage, so a pair of items that share one co-rater is not treated as evidence.

      User-user collaborative filtering

      People like you liked this. Adds diversity that item-item misses. Noisier on small data, which is why it never carries the most weight.

      Content-based (TF-IDF)

      This is about what you like. The only signal that works for a brand-new user, and the only one that can explain itself in words.

      The blend adapts

      Weights depend on two things: how many ratings you have, and how much your ratings vary. Collaborative filtering works on deviations from your own mean — so if you rate everything 5.0, your deviation is zero and the collaborative signal carries no information. The blend detects that and hands the weight to content instead of pretending otherwise.

      What this is not

      The catalogue and the rating history are generated. The engine is not: it is the same code that would run on real data, evaluated honestly against a baseline. The evaluation had a genuine bug when it was first written — see the README.
